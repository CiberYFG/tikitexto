European Cup and Champions League statistics 1956–2026: winners by club, country and decade

Data as of 24 November 2026

TIKITEXTO's own study of the 71 European Cup and Champions League winners: titles by club and country, streaks, droughts, one-time winners and who dominates each decade. With a filterable table.

The key findings in 7 numbers

  • Real Madrid has won 15 of the 71 editions (21 %), more than double the next club, Milan (7).
  • Only 24 clubs have won the tournament in 71 editions, and the top three alone account for 28 titles (39 %).
  • Spain leads by country with 20 titles, ahead of England (15) and Italy (12).
  • The longest run by a club is 5 titles in a row (Real Madrid, 1956–1960); by a country, 6 (England, 1977–1982).
  • The longest drought between two titles for the same club was Inter's: 45 years (1965–2010).
  • 10 clubs won once and have not repeated: Celtic, Feyenoord, Aston Villa, Hamburg, Steaua Bucharest, PSV Eindhoven, Red Star Belgrade, Marseille, Borussia Dortmund, Manchester City.
  • The last champion from outside the big five countries (Spain, England, Italy, Germany and France) was Porto in 2004.

Titles per club (71 editions)

The 10 one-time winners show how closed the roll of honour is: of the 24 champions, 8 have three or more cups and hold 49 of the 71 titles.

Champions from outside the big five countries

Between 1956 and 2026 there were 13 titles won by clubs from countries other than Spain, England, Italy, Germany and France: Benfica, Celtic, Feyenoord, Ajax, Steaua, Porto, PSV and Red Star. Since 2004, the last year with a champion from outside the group, the next 22 titles have all gone to those five countries.

How we made this study

Every figure is calculated by a program from the list of European Cup (1956–1992) and Champions League (from 1993) winners that we use on our champions pages, checked against UEFA.com. A “drought” is the time between two consecutive titles of the same club; the “wait” is counted up to 2026. Each country is assigned by club (Red Star in 1991 stands for Yugoslavia). The interpretation is TIKITEXTO's; if you spot a mistake, write to us from the contact page.

Frequently asked questions

Who has won the most European Cups and Champions Leagues?

Real Madrid, with 15 titles in 71 editions; next come Milan (7) and, with 6 each, Bayern Munich and Liverpool.

Which country has the most Champions League titles?

Spain, with 20 titles, followed by England (15) and Italy (12).

What is the longest run of consecutive titles?

Five: Real Madrid won the first five editions (1956–1960). Since then Ajax (1971–1973), Bayern (1974–1976) and Real Madrid (2016–2018) have each won three in a row.

How many different clubs have been European champions?

24 clubs in 71 editions. Of them, 10 have won only once: Celtic, Feyenoord, Aston Villa, Hamburg, Steaua Bucharest, PSV Eindhoven, Red Star Belgrade, Marseille, Borussia Dortmund, Manchester City.

When did a club from outside the big five countries last win?

In 2004, with Porto. Since then every champion has come from Spain, England, Italy, Germany or France.
