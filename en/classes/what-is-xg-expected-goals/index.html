What the lesson explains

You've surely heard xG on a broadcast or on social media. It looks like a secret code, but it is very easy to understand. Let's see it step by step.

xG means expected goals. It is the probability that a shot ends in a goal, measured as a number between zero and one. The higher it is, the better the chance.

That number depends on several things: distance to goal, the angle of the shot, which part of the body it is struck with and how the play developed. A penalty is around zero point seven six. A shot from very far, barely zero point zero three.

If you add up the xG of all of a team's shots in a match, you get how many goals it would have scored on average from those chances. It is a way of measuring the quality of chances, not just the result.

That is why a team can lose and have played better. Here, the blue team created more dangerous chances than the red one, but lost. A match can be decided by a detail, but in the long run chances tend to win out.

There are more useful statistics: possession, shots on target, passes that lead to a shot, or xA, expected assists. None tells the whole story, but together they give a good picture of the match.

That said, xG has limits. It does not measure the quality of the striker or the goalkeeper, different companies use different models, and in a single match there is a lot of luck. It is more useful when looked at over many matches.

Now, when you hear that someone won by the narrowest margin but lost on xG, you'll know what they mean. It's a clue to see beyond the score and to understand the game better.
