Star Route: guess the player from the cities of his clubs

Star Route turns a footballer's career into a journey across the map. Each round shows two, three or more red pins, one for each city of the big clubs a legend played for, and four names to choose from. Only thirteen top European clubs count (Real Madrid, Barcelona, Atlético, Bayern, Juventus, Milan, Inter, Manchester United, Manchester City, Liverpool, Chelsea, Arsenal and PSG), so the challenge is working out who has exactly that itinerary: for example, a player who passed through Milan, Paris and Manchester. If you are unsure, there is an optional hint that tells you which national team he played for and in which position, at the cost of 40% of the points.

How to play

  1. Press “Today's challenge” (the same for everyone, one attempt a day) or “Free game” to practise.
  2. Look at the pins on the map: each one is the city of one or two of the thirteen clubs on the list. The map frames itself so all of them are visible.
  3. Think about which player wore the shirts of those cities. If a city has two clubs, either of them counts.
  4. Tap the name you think is right. If you doubt, press “Hint” to see national team and position, although a correct answer will be worth 40% less.
  5. When you answer, the clubs the player passed through are shown. Press “Next” until the five rounds are done.

Rules and scoring

  • A game is five rounds, ordered from most to fewest pins: long routes, which fit fewer players, come first.
  • Each round lasts 28 seconds. A correct answer is worth between 500 and 1,000 points depending on speed; with the hint used, 60% of that figure. A miss scores 0 and there is only one attempt.
  • Only the clubs on the closed list of thirteen count; other teams the player went through do not appear on the map. Madrid, Milan, Manchester and London have two clubs each, and either one counts.
  • The four options never have exactly the same set of cities as the answer, so each round has a single correct solution. The other options usually share a city, a position or a national team so it is not trivial.
  • In the daily challenge you earn a tikitexto if your average is above 500 points per round; in free games, one every three games with an average above 600.

Tips to play better

  • Start with the oddest city: if there is a pin in Turin or Munich, you can immediately rule out every player who did not go through Juventus or Bayern.
  • Count the pins: a route with three or four cities only fits much-travelled players, such as those who have played in several countries.
  • Remember that two clubs share a city: a pin in Milan does not tell Milan from Inter, nor one in London Chelsea from Arsenal.
  • Use the hint when you are torn between two names: knowing the national team and the position is often enough to decide, and 600 points beat 0.
  • Think about the era: if you doubt between a retired legend and a current player, consider which route each one could have followed.

Frequently asked questions

Why only thirteen clubs?

Because the game needs to know for certain which clubs each player went through so there is a single correct answer. With a closed list the data is complete and verifiable; that is why only the thirteen biggest clubs on the continent are used.

Why doesn't a famous player appear?

Only players who passed through at least two cities on the list are asked as the answer, and only those in TIKITEXTO's database, which grows little by little.

What does the hint show?

The national team the footballer played for and his position. Using it reduces that round's score by 40%.

Is it the same challenge for everyone?

Yes. Today's challenge is generated from the local date, so everyone gets the same players and options, with one attempt a day.

Is time counted?

Yes: each round lasts 28 seconds and the sooner you answer, the more points. If time runs out, the round scores 0.
