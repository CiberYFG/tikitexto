Pin the Spot: the football map game with stadiums, birthplaces and finals

Pin the Spot is a geography game for football fans. Every round gives you a question — where a stadium is, where a star was born, which city a club plays in, where a final was held — and you have to drop a pin on the right spot of a world map. The map can be dragged and zoomed with your finger, the mouse wheel or the zoom buttons, and as you get closer more cities, roads and names appear, just like on a real map. The closer your pin lands to the real place, the more points you earn: up to 1,000 per round. A game is five rounds, and today's challenge always brings one of each kind (a stadium, a star's birthplace, a club, a final and a fun fact), the same for everyone, with a single attempt per day. There is also a free game, with a category filter and an expert mode with no names on the map.

How to play

  1. Press “Today's challenge” (the same for everyone, one attempt a day) or “Free game” to practise without limits.
  2. Read the round's question: it can be a stadium, a footballer's birthplace, a club's city, the venue of a final or a fun fact.
  3. Pan the map with your finger or mouse and zoom in (pinch, wheel or the +/− buttons) until you find the area.
  4. Tap the map to place your pin; if you doubt it, tap another spot and it moves. When you are sure, press “Lock it in”.
  5. You will see the real place, the line joining it to your pin, the error in kilometres and the round's points. After five rounds the result appears with a card to share.

Rules and scoring

  • A game is five rounds. In the daily challenge there is one round of each kind — stadium, star's birthplace, club, final and fun fact — ordered from easiest to hardest; in the free game you can pick one category or mix them all.
  • Each round's score depends only on the straight-line distance between your pin and the real place. Inside a small margin (about 2.5 km for stadiums and 6 to 8 km for the rest) you get the full 1,000 points; beyond it the score falls gradually, so missing by a few kilometres barely costs anything and missing by a continent scores almost nothing.
  • The hint shows a rough area about 1,300 km across that contains the place, but it takes 40% off that round's points. It can only be used once per round.
  • Expert mode, available in the free game, removes the names of countries, cities and seas from the map and multiplies your points by 1.5. It does not apply to the daily challenge, so that everyone plays under the same conditions.
  • If you complete the daily challenge with an average of at least 500 points per round you earn +1 tikitext, and it counts towards your daily streak. In the free game you earn one tikitext for every three games with an average of 600 or more. The result is shared as a row of five coloured squares, without revealing the places.

Tips to play better

  • Zoom in before you tap: a pin placed with the map zoomed far out can miss by hundreds of kilometres without you noticing. Get close enough to see the coast, the rivers or the neighbouring cities.
  • Use the cities that appear as you zoom in as references. Stadiums are usually on the outskirts or next to an access road, so the urban area places you within a few kilometres.
  • For birthplaces and clubs, think about the region: many footballers were born in medium-sized towns near a big capital. Locating the capital well and shifting a little already earns lots of points.
  • Keep the hint for rounds where you have no idea about the continent: it lowers the points, but spares you an error of thousands of kilometres that would cost far more.
  • Once you master the map, try expert mode with no names: it forces you to recognise coastlines and borders by heart and multiplies what you earn by 1.5.

Frequently asked questions

How are the points calculated?

The straight-line distance between your pin and the real place is measured. Inside a small margin (about 2.5 km for stadiums, 6 to 8 km for the rest) you get 1,000 points; further away, the score falls gradually. The hint takes off 40% and expert mode multiplies by 1.5.

Is it the same challenge for every player?

Yes. Today's challenge is generated from the local date, so everyone plays the same five places and can compare their result with their friends. There is only one attempt per day.

Where do the maps come from?

The map is drawn with Natural Earth data, a public-domain cartographic dataset. We gathered the coordinates of stadiums, birthplaces and final venues and checked them against official sources such as UEFA, FIFA and the clubs' websites.

What does each colour in the result mean?

Each square sums up one round: green if you landed within a few kilometres, yellow if you were close, orange if you missed by a region and red if you picked the wrong area. It is a way to share how you did without giving away the answers.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes. You drag the map with one finger, zoom by pinching and place the pin with a tap. There are also zoom buttons for those who prefer them.
