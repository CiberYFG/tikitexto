Crosses: the 3×3 football grid with players, clubs and national teams

Crosses is a football grid game: three clubs on the rows, three conditions on the columns and nine squares to fill. In each square you must type a player who wore the shirt of that row's club and who also meets that column's condition: having played for a given national team, having won the Ballon d'Or, being a world champion or playing in a given position. The same player can only be used once, so sometimes it pays to leave the hardest square for last. Every grid is generated and checked to have a solution with different players, so it will never ask for something impossible. There is a daily challenge, the same for everyone and with a single attempt, and a free mode to practise as often as you like. The game ends when you miss three times or when you complete all nine squares.

How to play

  1. Press “Today's challenge” (the same for everyone, one attempt a day) or “Free mode” to practise.
  2. Tap a square: a text box with suggestions opens.
  3. Type a player's name and pick him from the list; he must have played for the row's club and meet the column's condition.
  4. If he fits, the square is filled; if not, you lose one of your three balls.
  5. Complete all nine squares before you run out of balls. If you get stuck, use one of the two hints.

Rules and scoring

  • Every row is a club and every column a condition: a national team, the Ballon d'Or, being a world champion or a position (goalkeeper, defender, midfielder or forward).
  • A player is valid in a square if he played for that club and meets that condition. Only players from the game's list count, which is the one the search box suggests while you type.
  • Each player can only be used in one square. If you repeat one, the game warns you without taking a ball away.
  • You have three balls: every name that does not fit costs one. If you reach zero the game ends and the possible answers are shown. If the remaining squares run out of free players, the game also ends.
  • The daily challenge is generated from the date, so it is the same for everyone and you only get one attempt. Completing it gives +1 tikitext, and in free mode you also earn one for every three grids completed. It also counts towards your daily streak.

Tips to play better

  • Start with the most restrictive squares: the ones combining a club with “world champion” or “Ballon d'Or” usually have very few answers.
  • Think of players who passed through several clubs: one name can fit several squares, but you can only use it once, so save the versatile ones.
  • Before typing, check that the player meets both conditions at once; it is the most common mistake.
  • Use the hints once you have ruled out the obvious: they give you the player's initial and save you a mistake.
  • If you play the daily challenge, take your time: you only get one attempt and you will share the result with your friends.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't I type any player?

Because the game only knows the players on its list (about three hundred from the big leagues and national teams). That way it can guarantee that every grid has a solution and that the right answer is always recognised.

Can I repeat a player?

No: each player is only valid in one square. If you try, the game tells you and you do not lose a ball.

Is the daily challenge the same for everyone?

Yes. It is generated from the local date, so everyone plays the same grid and you can compare your result with your friends.

How many mistakes can I make?

Three. Every name that does not fit the chosen square takes away a ball.

What do the “Ballon d'Or” and “World champion” columns mean?

That the player has won the Ballon d'Or at some point or has been a world champion with his national team. They are based on the official records of France Football and FIFA.
